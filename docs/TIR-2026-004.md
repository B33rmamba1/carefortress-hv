## Executive Summary

For the first two weeks of CareFortress honeypot operations (July 13-26, 2026), every observed interaction with the stolen AWS canary credentials was reconnaissance: identity validation, service enumeration, and account-structure discovery. Reports TIR-2026-002 and TIR-2026-003 documented that reconnaissance ecosystem — a credential marketplace serving spam, AI-compute-theft, and account-recon buyer segments across nine distinct toolkits.

On July 27-28, 2026, that changed. Within a 24-hour window, the honeypot captured the first two "beyond reconnaissance" actions in the entire dataset: an IAM persistence attempt (CreateUser) and actual service abuse (SES-SMTP send attempts). This report documents the shift from credential validation to credential exploitation.

All attempts failed because the credentials are zero-permission Thinkst canary tokens. The value is in the captured intent and technique.

The same credentials cycling from recon to active send abuse illustrates the full lifecycle of a leaked key inside the marketplace: harvested -> validated -> assessed -> exploited.

---

## Analysis — Why the Shift Now

Two weeks of pure reconnaissance followed by two exploitation attempts in 24 hours is consistent with marketplace propagation: a credential first circulates among validators and assessors, then reaches end-users whose objective is exploitation. The delay between harvest and exploitation reflects the credential moving down the supply chain.

The two exploitation types map to two of the three buyer segments from TIR-2026-002:
- **CreateUser** -> account-takeover / persistence objective
- **SES-SMTP** -> spam-infrastructure objective (the send action the SES-recon segment was preparing for)

---

## Detection Recommendations

1. Alert on IAM write actions (CreateUser, CreateAccessKey, AttachUserPolicy, PutUserPolicy) from any credential with a read-only history — high-signal persistence indicator.
2. Distinguish SES API reconnaissance from SES-SMTP usage. GetSendQuota/GetSMSAttributes are assessment; actual SMTP sends are abuse.
3. Treat credential exposure as a multi-week risk, not a point-in-time event. These credentials were harvested weeks before exploitation.
4. Correlate action escalation per credential. A key moving GetCallerIdentity -> GetAccount -> CreateUser over days is a single actor progressing toward takeover.
